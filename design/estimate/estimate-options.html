Three ways to design the estimate

All three show the same brickmason estimate for Yusupovlar uyi:

  • Bricks: 5 800 pcs × 1 450.
  • Cement: 60 bags × 62 000.
  • Sand: 6 m³ × 180 000.
  • Reinforcing mesh: 40 m², no price yet.
  • Bricklaying labour: 4 500 000 as one agreed sum.

The total is 17 710 000 so‘m. As the spec requires, every quantity is typed by the builder and never changes on its own.

Hard to compare rowsEach row shows the total big and "qty × price" small underneath, so you can't scan quantities down a column.
Missing values hideA row with no price just shows 0. Nothing points it out before you issue the quote.
Retyping the same materialsBricks, cement and sand are typed again for every project.
Areas are a separate sheetThe measured wall area sits behind a link, so you copy it by hand into the quantity.
A

Table

A compact table like a paper BOM: Name | Qty | Price | Sum. Missing values show in amber; "+ Add row" sits under each section.

Good for: scanning and checking numbers down a column, like the paper BOMs brickmasons already use.

Good for: missing prices are impossible to miss.

Trade-off: narrow columns; long material names wrap to two lines.

B

Cards + quick add

A row of your own recent materials, with their last unit and price, adds a row in one tap. Each row is a card: name and sum, then "qty × price" as tappable chips.

C

Measured quantities at hand

The project's measured areas sit at the top of the estimate. When you edit a quantity, buttons offer those areas ("112,62 m² net wall"), and the quantity changes only when you tap one.
